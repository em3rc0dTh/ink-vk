## What Ink-VK means by Design DNA

In Ink-VK, **Design DNA is the reusable structural or behavioral intelligence that remains after removing a donor's branded expression.**

It is not the donor's colors, copy, typography, illustrations, proprietary assets, or pixel geometry.

Example:

```text
Dayos yellow + Suisse + 3D cubes
        = BRAND EXPRESSION

Sparse accent color used to encode emphasis
        = DESIGN DNA
```

The objective is therefore not to reproduce Dayos. It is to understand **why certain compositions work**, preserve the transferable principle, and later decide through a Replication / Transformation Contract whether that principle deserves to survive inside VTKALL.

---

# DNA-01 — Surface-state handoffs make section boundaries legible

Dayos does not merely place Section B below Section A. It frequently changes visual state through black, white, warm gray, inversion, and large rounded surface entries.

The transition itself communicates:

> a new thought has begun.

### Why it matters

A section boundary is easier to perceive when the handoff is deliberately composed instead of being produced only by vertical spacing.

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

This directly addresses recovered cases where previous, current and next sections compete within the same visual state.

### What not to copy

Do not copy Dayos's exact black/white palette or rounded geometry. The transferable intelligence is **explicit state transition**, not the visual skin.

---

# DNA-02 — Controlled peeking is different from accidental leakage

Seeing part of the next section is not inherently a failure.

It works when:

1. the current narrative unit has already resolved;
2. the next state enters deliberately;
3. the transition is visually understandable.

### Why it matters

This refines an early VTKALL assumption.

The stronger rule is not:

> Never show the next section.

It is:

> **Never allow several unresolved narrative states to compete.**

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

### What not to copy

Do not force artificial previews between sections. Peeking only works when it has narrative purpose.

---

# DNA-03 — Density follows purpose

Dayos does not use one universal density rule.

Examples:

```text
Narrative / manifesto
    → spacious

Use-case library
    → dense

Question matrix
    → dense

Process explanation
    → structured
```

### Why it matters

Whitespace is not automatically good, and density is not automatically bad. Composition should respond to the user's information job.

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

This is directly relevant to the recovered VTKALL contradiction of excessive whitespace coexisting with cramped content.

### What not to copy

Do not impose Dayos spacing values globally.

---

# DNA-04 — Wide visual field + narrow reading column

Dayos often keeps prose relatively narrow while using the broader viewport for a concrete secondary role:

- UI proof;
- imagery;
- metrics;
- comparison;
- card groups;
- process geometry.

### Why it matters

Readable text width does not require wasting the rest of the screen.

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

This directly attacks the recovered VTKALL pattern:

> content cramped in the center while large lateral space remains unused.

### What not to copy

Do not stretch content horizontally just to fill space. The remaining field needs a semantic job.

---

# DNA-05 — Family shells create consistency without identical pages

Hero Answers, Hero Actions and Hero Experts clearly belong to one page family.

They share structural grammar while changing their specific proposition, proof and visual payload.

### Why it matters

Consistency does not require cloning one page and replacing the text.

A route family can preserve:

```text
shared shell
shared hierarchy
shared handoffs
shared closure
```

while allowing each capability to prove itself differently.

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

### What not to copy

Do not mechanically create one universal VTKALL page template. Family contracts should follow real content relationships.

---

# DNA-06 — Local orientation is separate from global navigation

Long Dayos pages introduce a small local orientation rail while leaving the global navigation compact.

Examples include:

```text
Mission
First Principles
Partners
The Team
```

### Why it matters

Global navigation should not absorb every within-page navigation problem.

### VTKALL relevance

**CONTEXTUAL**

Useful only when a VTKALL route genuinely contains enough internal depth to justify local orientation.

### What not to copy

Do not add side rails merely because Dayos has them.

---

# DNA-07 — Typography behaves as geometry

Dayos's large condensed headings are not simply large labels.

They create visual mass, balance, hierarchy and rhythm.

### Why it matters

Typography can participate in composition as a spatial object.

### VTKALL relevance

**CONTEXTUAL**

The principle is transferable. Dayos's exact all-caps condensed voice is not.

### What not to copy

- Suisse typography;
- exact sizes;
- exact line-height;
- exact uppercase treatment.

---

# DNA-08 — Surface contrast replaces decorative elevation

Dayos obtains hierarchy mainly through:

- canvas changes;
- black/white inversion;
- card surfaces;
- large radii;
- contrast.

It does not depend heavily on shadows or ornamental depth.

### Why it matters

Hierarchy can be produced structurally rather than decoratively.

### VTKALL relevance

**CONTEXTUAL**

### What not to copy

Do not turn this into a rule that VTKALL can never use shadows, gradients or other depth devices. The transferable idea is disciplined hierarchy.

---

# DNA-09 — Proof sits directly behind the claim

A strong Dayos proposition is commonly followed by concrete evidence:

```text
CLAIM
  ↓
MECHANISM / EXAMPLE
  ↓
PROOF
```

Proof devices include:

- product UI;
- metrics;
- ecosystem marks;
- security evidence;
- comparison;
- operational examples.

### Why it matters

The user does not need to retain an unsupported promise across several unrelated sections before receiving evidence.

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

### What not to copy

Do not reuse Dayos metrics, screenshots, partner marks or evidence.

---

# DNA-10 — The visual device follows the semantic job

Dayos does not treat every information problem as another generic card.

Recovered relationships include:

| Information job | Visual device |
|---|---|
| Category identity | 3D object |
| Product mechanism | UI screenshot/demo |
| Magnitude | Metric |
| Trade-off | Comparison table |
| Discovery | Filter + card matrix |
| People/company reality | Photography |
| Sequential process | Connected timeline |

### Why it matters

Representation should reinforce meaning.

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

### What not to copy

Do not adopt Dayos's 3D objects or proprietary visual metaphors. Recover the semantic relationship instead.

---

# DNA-11 — Sequential information becomes spatial sequence

When the information itself contains order, Dayos sometimes expresses that order geometrically.

Observed donor controls:

```text
PLANS
DIAGNOSE → SCOPE → BUILD → SHIP

PARTNERSHIP
APPLY → LAUNCH PILOT → PROVE VALUE → SCALE
```

### Why it matters

The user perceives progression from the layout itself rather than reconstructing order from unrelated stacked blocks.

### VTKALL relevance

**VERY HIGH VALUE**

This is the strongest external evidence supporting the earlier VTKALL `DEMONSTRATIONS-03` sequence hypothesis.

### What not to copy

Do not copy the four-step Dayos component. Preserve only the principle that **ordered information may deserve ordered spatial representation**.

---

# DNA-12 — Stable global frame + compact disclosure

Dayos can expose Platform, Solutions, Resources or Company sub-navigation while maintaining the overall global navigation frame and primary CTA.

### Why it matters

Disclosure adds context without replacing the user's current navigational frame.

### VTKALL relevance

**CONTEXTUAL**

### What not to copy

Do not reproduce the exact pill navbar or dropdown styling unless later justified by VTKALL's own Soul and interaction contract.

---

# DNA-13 — Page closure is an explicit state

Dayos frequently treats route closure as its own visual moment:

```text
content
   ↓
closing CTA
   ↓
footer
```

The CTA and footer do not accidentally collapse into one unresolved composition.

### Why it matters

The route gets a clear final action before the navigational/footer state begins.

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

This directly maps to several recovered VTKALL frames where previous section + CTA + footer were visible simultaneously.

### What not to copy

Do not assume the exact dual-CTA composition is appropriate for every VTKALL route.

---

# DNA-14 — Accent color has a job

Dayos uses strong chromatic accents comparatively sparingly.

Color becomes meaningful because it is not everywhere.

It can encode:

- emphasis;
- selection;
- category;
- action;
- highlighted state.

### Why it matters

Scarcity gives accent color semantic weight.

### VTKALL relevance

**CONTEXTUAL**

### What not to copy

Do not adopt Dayos yellow or mint as VTKALL colors.

The transferable principle is:

> **Accent color should carry a job, not merely decoration.**

---

# DNA-15 — Coherence does not require one page template

This is the broadest Dayos DNA recovered.

The donor uses multiple page modes:

```text
NARRATIVE MODE
Home / Company

FAMILY DETAIL MODE
Hero Answers / Actions / Experts

DENSE-PROOF MODE
Solutions

LIBRARY MODE
Use Cases

DECISION + PROCESS MODE
Plans / Partnership

SINGLE-TASK MODE
Schedule Demo
```

These routes differ substantially in information architecture while still feeling like one system.

### Why it matters

A coherent product is not necessarily a product where every page is built from the same template.

Coherence can come from shared grammar:

- hierarchy;
- surfaces;
- typography roles;
- interaction patterns;
- handoffs;
- spacing logic;
- action grammar;
- proof grammar.

### VTKALL relevance

**HIGH VALUE**

### What not to copy

Do not use Dayos page modes as mandatory VTKALL route types. They are donor evidence, not target architecture.
